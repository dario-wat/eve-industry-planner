EVE market and trading tool — greenfield build plan

Thirty-seven proposed tools across seven layers for a station-trading-first market analysis and prediction application, ordered so each layer depends only on the ones above it. Game mechanics verified against CCP documentation and the EVE University wiki as of September 2026.

The decision that determines your ceiling

ESI gives you a live order book cached for five minutes, and a once-daily price summary. There is nothing in between, and the gap is permanent — you cannot backfill it later. If you poll the book and persist the diff between consecutive polls, you reconstruct order lifecycles, per-side traded volume, and competitor repricing behaviour, none of which any ESI-only tool can see. If you do not, you are permanently limited to restating what the in-game market window already shows.

Ingest cadence and cost

The order-book route returns at most 1,000 orders per page, so one full pull of a region costs one request per page. The Forge currently holds roughly 419,000 public orders, which is about 419 requests per full pull. That is the unit of cost for everything below.

Rate limits are not the constraint

Since 24 February 2026 the market-order route group is rate limited to 12,000 tokens per rolling 15-minute window, with a successful response costing 2 tokens and tokens returning to the bucket after 15 minutes. CCP sized this deliberately: their announcement notes that all 113 regions together come to 1,723 pages, and that the budget is "enough to fetch every region's orders every 5 minutes if you really wanted to" — which works out to 1,723 × 3 cycles × 2 tokens = 10,338 tokens against the 12,000 limit.

Polling The Forge alone at full cadence costs 419 × 3 × 2 = 2,514 tokens per window, about a fifth of the budget. You will not hit the rate limit with any sane design.

Storage is the constraint, and only if you store snapshots

The two figures commonly cited both refer to storing complete order books repeatedly:

  • A developer archiving all EVE market data reported on the official forums that each compressed snapshot of The Forge was 5.9 MB, and at 288 snapshots per day that came to roughly 1.7 GB/day for that one region. That measurement dates from when The Forge held about 285,000 orders; scaled to today's ~419,000 it would be closer to 2.5 GB/day.
  • An independent write-up measured a single order-book page at 306 KB of JSON, putting one uncompressed snapshot of The Forge at ~112 MB and estimating 72 GB/day uncompressed across the major trade hubs at 5-minute cadence.

Both numbers describe the approach this plan explicitly avoids. Storing diffs instead changes the arithmetic entirely: between two consecutive polls most orders are byte-identical, so you write only created, repriced, partially-consumed, and vanished orders. The steady-state write volume becomes a function of market churn rather than market size. Measure your actual churn rate in the first week and size storage from that — I would not trust anyone's estimate, including mine, over a direct measurement on your own data.

Choosing a cadence

You do not have to poll every five minutes, and for most regions you should not. What degrades as you slow down is not price accuracy — it is your ability to see events between polls. An order that is created and fully consumed inside one interval is invisible no matter what you do, so the interval sets a floor on what you can ever know.

Recommended: tier the cadence by region, and always pull whole regions

Fetching a whole region is dramatically cheaper per item than fetching types individually. One full Forge pull costs ~419 requests and returns every tradeable type; fetching 500 watchlist types one at a time costs ~500 requests and returns only those 500. Once your watchlist exceeds a few hundred items, whole-region pulls win outright — so scope by region and cadence, never by type.

A sensible split: your home trading region at 5 or 10 minutes, because that is where the competition and per-side-volume signal actually pays; the other hub regions hourly, which is ample for cross-region arbitrage where you are comparing prices rather than watching order flow; everything else daily or not at all. Daily history is a separate, trivially cheap job: one request per type per region per day, and it can be missed without permanent loss because CCP serves it back to the start of the previous calendar year.

Mechanics that invalidate naive tools

Each of these breaks an assumption most market tools make implicitly. Worth encoding as tests rather than remembering.

TrapWhat it means for the design
Daily history is region-wideThere is no per-station history endpoint. For The Forge, Jita dominates enough that it is a fair proxy; for most other regions it silently blends several unrelated markets.
Structure orders are invisible by default/markets/{region_id}/orders/ excludes player structures except for ranged orders. Each citadel must be fetched separately with esi-markets.structure_markets.v1 and docking access, then merged.
Best price is often one unit deepRanking on top-of-book is the single biggest source of false positives. Walk the book for the quantity you actually intend to trade.
Buy orders have range and minimum volumeA regional buy order only matches sellers within its range, and a min-volume order will not fill small sales. Both fields must be respected before counting an order as liquidity.
Order modification has a five-minute cooldownThis drives order layering and constrains any automated repricing logic.
Daily downtime distorts intraday statisticsRoughly 11:00–11:30 UTC every market goes silent. Exclude the window from activity and cadence metrics.
Character order history only goes back 90 daysYour own long-term record has to be accumulated by you. Start persisting wallet and order data before you need it.
Most online guides are staleBroker Relations gives 0.3%/level, not 0.1%. Sales tax base is 7.5%, not 4% or 2%. Margin Trading no longer exists. Guides predating 2025 get all three wrong.

Suggested build order

PhaseFocusContents and rationale
1Substrate onlyBook snapshotter and differ, history warehouse, quote series, SDE service. Ship no UI. Every week you delay is a week of history you can never recover.
2Fee engine and item pageFee resolver, profitability calculator, depth-aware pricer, item deep-dive workbench. First genuinely useful screen, and it validates that the substrate is correct.
3ScannersLiquidity classifier, station-trading scanner, competition meter, volume-balance screen, spike detector. By now you have enough stored history for these to be meaningful.
4Workflow and P&LOrder monitor, relist decision engine, FIFO cost basis, realized P&L, daily worklist. This is where the tool starts saving you hours rather than just informing you.
5Prediction and validationFill-probability model, margin persistence, backtester, scorecard. Needs several months of accumulated substrate to be worth anything at all.

If you only build three things

  • The order-book differ, because it is the only irreversible decision here — history you do not record today cannot be recovered tomorrow.
  • The volume-balance screen, because buy-side versus sell-side split is the highest-signal filter available and ESI will never give it to you directly.
  • The relist cost model, because recurring relist fees on full order value are where station-trading profit actually disappears, and essentially no public tool accounts for them.

Those three, plus a fee resolver and a single item page, constitute a tool that is already better informed than anything you can buy or browse.
